<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| stored `prospects.website_url` → outbound fetch | A third-party URL stored at import time, re-fetched later for robots.txt and then crawled |
| Next.js library → scanner-service | Authenticated service call carrying a caller-chosen crawl identity |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-04-08 | Tampering / Elevation of Privilege | `dispatchClaimedProspects()` outbound fetches | high | mitigate | Every prospect's `website_url` is re-run through `validateUrlSafe()` (`lib/url-validation.server.ts`) before the robots pre-flight and before dispatch — a URL that passed validation at import time can have been repointed since, so stored-is-safe is not assumed (SSRF) |
| T-04-09 | Repudiation | failure and skip reasons | low | mitigate | `markScanFailed()` always records a reason in `prospects.scan_status_reason`, so a robots skip is distinguishable from a dispatch failure after the fact (D-10) |
| T-04-10 | Denial of Service | third-party sites and the shared Railway IP | high | mitigate | `pLimit(BULK_DISPATCH_CONCURRENCY)` bounds in-flight dispatches and `BULK_DISPATCH_SPACING_MS` spaces them within a tick, so a tick paces rather than bursts (Pitfall 2, SCAN-06) |
| T-04-11 | Tampering | public scanner rate-limit counters | medium | mitigate | Bulk `scans` rows are written under the `BULK_SCAN_IP_HASH` sentinel, never a real or hashed visitor IP, so bulk volume cannot consume a real visitor's hourly scan allowance |
</threat_model>
